Touch typing rests on a simple idea: every finger owns its own part of the keyboard, so there is no need to look at the keys. The fingers rest on the home row, the index fingers find the bumps on F and J, and the thumbs hover over the space bar.

At first it feels slower. That is true, but only for a while! After a week of calm practice your hands learn the way on their own, and after a month your speed passes the old one while your eyes stay on the text.

The main rule is that accuracy beats speed. A mistake fixed at once costs two or three extra keystrokes; a habit of mistakes costs months of relearning. So type evenly, without bursts, and do not rush: the pace will come by itself.

Short daily sessions are better than rare long ones. Fifteen minutes a day, 7 days a week, give more than two hours on a Sunday. Take breaks, mind your posture and keep your wrists relaxed.

Once the letters feel steady, add capitals, punctuation and digits: Shift is pressed with the little finger of the other hand, and the question mark sits next to the full stop. Sounds hard? In a couple of days it becomes a habit.
